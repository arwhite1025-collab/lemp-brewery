Home of Booze, Blues & Shoes

The Lemp Brewery Complex is the manufacturing legacy of two of the most successful businesses in American history — the William J. Lemp Brewing Company and the International Shoe Company (ISCO). Together, they defined St. Louis's identity as the home of "Booze, Blues and Shoes" and left behind one of the most architecturally significant industrial campuses in the country.

The story begins in 1838, when Adam Lemp — the first person in the United States to produce lager-style beer — started a small brewing operation near the St. Louis riverfront, now the site of the Gateway Arch. In 1864, his son William J. Lemp moved operations to the current site, drawn by two natural advantages: underground limestone caves ideal for cold-aging beer before mechanical refrigeration existed, and proximity to the Mississippi River, from which workers cut ice each winter to pack the caverns. Over the next 55 years, the Lemp family built their brewery into an industry giant — eventually surpassing their neighbor Anheuser-Busch to become the largest brewery in St. Louis and one of the largest in the nation, with celebrated brands including Falstaff, Lemp Standard Lager, and Culmbacher.

The Lemps operated their own refrigerated railroad — the Western Cable Railway Company — and were the first in the nation to establish coast-to-coast beer distribution, with sales offices in most major U.S. cities. By the 1890s their beers were being shipped around the globe, and in 1912 Lemp became the first brewery in the world to deliver beer by airplane. The family designed their buildings with architectural ambition to match their commercial success, and the complex became a symbol of civic wealth and power in St. Louis.

Prohibition ended the dynasty almost overnight. With brewing operations forced to shut down in 1919 and convinced that Prohibition would never be repealed, the Lemp family sold the complex at public auction on June 28, 1922. The Falstaff trademark was later sold to another St. Louis brewer — and proved so powerful that Falstaff continued to outsell Budweiser on its own home turf well into the 1960s.

Out of those ashes came a remarkable second chapter. International Shoe Company took ownership in 1922 and used every available square foot of the campus for shoe manufacturing, packaging, storage, and distribution — supplying over 90 other ISCO facilities across the country from this single site. With iconic brands like Poll Parrot, Red Goose, and Florsheim, ISCO grew into the nation's largest shoe manufacturer and by 1962 was one of the largest industrial employers of any kind in the United States, with over 33,000 personnel. In 1966, as the business diversified into retailing and apparel, ISCO changed its name to Interco. Through the 1970s and 80s, as shoe production moved overseas, manufacturing at the Lemp site wound down — and in the 1990s Interco began leasing the complex as a multi-tenant industrial campus, the use it carries forward to this day.

Nearly two centuries of American industry are written into every brick of this place. As St. Louis historian Stephen P. Walker put it: "The well-chosen architects and the fine St. Louis German bricklayers that created the Lemp Brewery were among the most elegant masters of their trades. As a result, the brewery was more than a workshop, more than a factory, more than an office — it was an outstanding ornament to the community."

In the Heart of the City

The Lemp Brewery Complex sits at the intersection of Cherokee Street and Lemp Avenue in South St. Louis — one of the city's most vibrant and walkable neighborhoods. Minutes from downtown with easy highway access and proximity to public transit.

The surrounding Cherokee Street corridor is home to a thriving mix of restaurants, galleries, and independent businesses, making the complex a natural destination for tenants who value character and community.
